SpatialDex

Reconstruction‑Augmented Visuomotor Pre‑Training of 3D‑Grounded Bimanual Dexterity from RGB‑Only Human Videos

Xingyi He* Zhiwen Qiu* Adhitya Polavaram* Yunhao Cao* Cory Fan Julius Samwer Saleh Kalantari Kuan Fang
* Equal contribution
Paper Code Dataset Video BibTeX

Abstract

We present SpatialDex, a framework that learns 3D-grounded bimanual dexterity from RGB-only human videos through reconstruction-augmented visuomotor pre-training. The central idea is to learn human behavior together with its spatial context. From each video, we reconstruct the scene geometry and estimate the hand motion, and align them at a shared video-level scale using the hand-size prior of 3D hand estimates. We retain the reconstructed geometry as both a policy input and a prediction target. The policy grounds the token grid of a pre-trained vision-language model in this geometry through gated residual fusion, is trained to predict future scene geometry alongside wrist and finger actions, and generates finger motion conditioned on the predicted trajectories of both wrists. Pre-trained on 1M human-video clips and fine-tuned with a small number of robot demonstrations, SpatialDex outperforms state-of-the-art methods by 16.5 points on nine simulated dexterous tasks across two embodiments and by 38.3 points on six real-world bimanual dexterous tasks. Under a matched comparison, pre-training with reconstructed geometry improves success by 14.2 points over pre-training on hand motion alone.

Highlights

  • 1M human-video clips with aligned scene geometry and hand motion. We reconstruct depth and camera motion for the clips of VITRA-1M and align them with the hand motion at a shared scale.
  • A 3D-grounded visuomotor policy pre-trained on human videos. The released checkpoint fine-tunes to bimanual dexterous tasks with a small number of robot demonstrations.
  • 43.3% → 81.7% success on six real-world bimanual tasks, from the strongest baseline π0.5 to SpatialDex.
  • 59.8% → 76.3% success on nine simulated tasks across two embodiments, from the strongest baseline to SpatialDex.
  • 58.9% → 73.1% success from pre-training on hand motion alone to pre-training with reconstructed geometry.

Aligned Scene and Hand Reconstruction

SpatialDex turns each RGB-only human video into paired supervision of scene geometry and hand motion.

Pre-Training Dataset

We release the reconstructed scene geometry for 1.16M clips of VITRA-1M, with a depth map and a camera for each of 31.7M frames estimated with VGGT-Ω. Each clip is packaged with the MANO hand poses and the per-hand instructions of VITRA-1M, so that geometry, motion, and language load together.

Contents of each frame. Every frame of a clip carries the fields below.

FieldContents
DepthA depth map of the scene at about 512² pixels, stored losslessly at millimeter precision.
CameraThe camera pose and intrinsics, which place every frame of a clip in one coordinate frame.
HandsMANO pose, shape, and 21 joints in 3D for the left and right hand, with a validity flag for each frame.
LanguageThe instruction that each hand is carrying out. The dataset holds 1.56M instruction segments, each with five rephrasings.
SourceThe video ID and frame index, used to decode the RGB frame from the original video.

Dataset composition by source. Hours are computed from the native frame rate of each source. RGB frames are not redistributed and are decoded from the original videos. Ego-Exo4D, which the paper also uses for pre-training, is not part of this release.

3D-Grounded Visuomotor Policy

The policy is pre-trained on this supervision and adapted to a robot with a small number of teleoperated demonstrations.

  • Grid-aligned geometry. Depth samples from each image patch are back-projected to metric 3D points, encoded jointly with a point-cloud encoder, and pooled back to their patches, so geometry lands on the same token grid as the VLM's visual features.
  • Progressive dual-gated fusion. Two sigmoid gates modulate appearance and geometry before a residual update whose weight warms up from zero, leaving the pre-trained visual priors intact early in training.
  • Forward dynamics prediction. A training-only predictor estimates future scene geometry from the shared context, so the representation must retain spatial structure that the action losses alone do not demand.
  • Coordinated wrist and finger experts. A wrist expert predicts both wrist trajectories, and a finger expert predicts finger actions conditioned on them.

Evaluation in Simulation

We evaluate nine simulated tasks on two embodiments. SpatialDex reaches 76.3% mean success, compared with 59.8% for the strongest baseline, π0.5, with the largest gains on tasks that require bimanual coordination.

DexJoCo Benchmark

Six DexJoCo tasks with Allegro hands on Franka arms. The first four tasks are unimanual, and Bimanual Assembly and Bimanual Microwave require coordinating both arms.

Our Wuji + Tianji Tasks

We introduce three bimanual tasks with Wuji hands on Tianji arms, the same embodiment as our real robot, to evaluate the policy on hands with more degrees of freedom.

What Drives the Gains

Under a matched comparison on the same videos, demonstrations, and budget, motion-only pre-training raises mean success from 45.6% to 58.9%, and adding reconstructed geometry raises it further to 73.1%. Removing any component of the geometry pathway or the coordinated action experts lowers success.
